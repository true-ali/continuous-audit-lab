# Limitations

This is a demonstration of a continuous-audit approach. It is not a compliance tool, and it has not
been validated against real fraud or error outcomes. The list below is the honest scope of what the
code and its results can and cannot support.

1. **The real data is unlabelled.** The South Lakeland payments file has no record of which payments
   were actually problems. There is therefore no accuracy, precision or recall claim for the real
   data. The real-data output is a ranked list for a person to review, and the spot-check in
   `SPOTCHECK.md` is the only check on it.

2. **Synthetic anomalies are rule-like, so the recall figures are optimistic.** The generator in
   `data/synthetic.py` injects anomalies with the same shapes the SQL tests look for. Duplicates are
   exact copies a few days later; split payments sit at 85% to 99% of a limit, inside the 80% to 100%
   band; round amounts are exact multiples of 1,000; off-hours postings fall on weekends or in night hours. A
   real fraud is less tidy. The measured recall of 0.946 for the risk score (precision@50 0.153) should
   be read as an upper bound for this design, not an estimate for a real ledger.

3. **Thresholds and weights are assumptions.** The approval limits (1,000 / 5,000 / 10,000), the
   windows, the round unit, the business hours, the risk weights and the KRI RAG rules are documented
   judgement calls in `tests_sql/params.py`, `scoring/risk.py` and `monitor/kri.py`. They were not fitted
   or calibrated against labels. The risk score is a ranking, not a probability. The approval limits are
   not the council's real limits.

4. **Benford does not fit every population.** Benford's law does not hold for bounded or assigned amounts,
   fixed fees, amounts capped at an approval limit, mostly round amounts, small samples or months
   dominated by one supplier (see the header of `benford_first_digit.sql`). The `benford_mad` KRI and any
   `nonconforming` grade are weak evidence on their own.

5. **The Benford "nonconforming" cut-off is unverified.** The bands 0.006, 0.012 and 0.015 are quoted in the
   test header from Nigrini (2012). The first three have been checked against a source. The cut-off above
   0.015 has **not** been independently verified in this repository and must be checked against the source
   before the label is relied on.

6. **threshold_splitting has known false positives on clean data.** On the clean synthetic ledger the test
   produces 4 findings (the test limit is 5). Ordinary high-volume invoices that fall in the 80% to 100%
   band can still form a cluster. Each finding needs a human check; the expected-rate correction reduces
   this but does not remove it.

7. **Only 3 dormant-supplier events exist in the synthetic data.** `dormant_reactivation` recall is 3 of 3,
   which is based on three injected events. That is too few to say anything about its precision or
   general recall.

8. **flagged_value KRI is red in every post-baseline month on the synthetic data.** The clean baseline has a
   median of 0 and the KRI has a floor of 1,000 on its scale, so the red limit is 3,000 and the baseline
   gives no spread to work with. The red status is a calibration artefact of the assumption, not a finding
   about the data. Red in 24 of 24 months should not be presented as a signal.

9. **Two injected anomaly types have no dedicated SQL test.** Structuring bursts (several payments of 100 to
   400 to one supplier) and pass-through pairs (a large inflow forwarded almost in full) are generated by
   `data/synthetic.py` but no SQL test targets them. They can reach the score only through the statistical
   components, and their recall was not measured separately.

10. **reference_integrity is not exercised by the synthetic data.** The synthetic references form one
    unbroken sequence, so the sequence-gap branch never fires there. The test only checks that the query
    runs. Its sequence-gap findings also list no transaction ids, so they do not affect the per-payment
    score or the value KRIs. The reused-reference branch is tested only for the absence of findings on clean
    data.

11. **No holiday calendar.** Public holidays are not modelled, so an off-hours flag on a holiday weekday is
    not raised, and a genuine holiday posting outside hours is raised as off-hours.

12. **Supplier matching is by name only.** A supplier is identified by the exact text of its name. Spelling
    variants, a renamed supplier, or two suppliers sharing one bank account are not linked. Related-party and
    shell-company patterns are therefore out of reach.

13. **Duplicate and split logic depends on exact field values.** Amounts must match to the penny and
    references must match exactly. A duplicate with a changed amount or reference is missed by the duplicate
    test. The synthetic data does not test this.

14. **Evidence re-performance has a gap for statistical-only alerts.** An alert that fired no row-level test
    is checked only by its evidence hash. The hash covers the involved rows and parameters, but not the
    weights or the risk score, so the score itself is not re-performed. The weights are stored in
    `params_json` but are excluded from the hash.

15. **The Isolation Forest is fitted and scored in-sample for its reference.** The baseline percentile is
    computed on the same rows the forest was fitted on. This is a simplification, not a held-out calibration.
    Expense-type baselines with fewer than 20 rows, and supplier histories with fewer than 5 earlier rows,
    give a z-score of 0 rather than a score.

16. **Statistical scores depend on a 12-month window.** The first 12 months are warm-up, so the first alerts
    the monitor can meaningfully score come after that, and early-period behaviour is not evaluated.

17. **Single run, single seed.** The headline numbers come from one synthetic ledger with seed 42 and default
    settings. No confidence intervals or sensitivity analysis over seeds or parameters have been run.

18. **Loader handling of malformed rows is not fully tested downstream.** Rows with unparseable amounts or
    dates are kept as NaN and counted in the quality report. How every downstream test treats those NaN values
    was not separately verified.

19. **Monthly granularity and a fixed alert budget.** Each month returns at most 50 alerts with a positive score,
    regardless of how many anomalies the month contains. A month with more real problems than that will have
    some of them cut off.

20. **Scale is untested.** The synthetic ledger has at least 14,400 clean rows (400 a month for 36 months,
    plus the injected anomalies), and the
    per-month monitor re-runs every SQL test on the cut-off ledger for each month. Runtime and memory on the full
    real file were not measured here, and the scoring code has a time-budget test only for the statistics step.

21. **Not a compliance or AML control.** Nothing here establishes compliance with anti-money-laundering rules,
    audit standards or any regulator's expectations. Its outputs are prompts for human review, not findings, and
    they must not be used as the basis for a decision about any supplier or person.
